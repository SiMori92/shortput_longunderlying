Garden study · Saint-Rémy and Brabant

UI Design Workbook

Worn in the tone of the asylum garden (1889) and Reminiscence of Brabant (1890), with a touch of Boulevard de Clichy (1887). Brabant gives the sage ground and its ochre contours; the garden gives the violet voice and the red earth; Clichy gives the short broken ticks. The brushwork is around the work, never under it.

§1

Design principles

Calm, capable, human. The garden is atmosphere and the chalk wall is the work: opaque surfaces, one primary action, reserved state colours. Brushwork frames content; it is never a texture under text or figures.

Clarity first

Persistent navigation, clear page identity, one prominent action per task area, readable work surfaces.

Honest data

Show limitations and failures beside the figures they qualify. Never hide a denominator.

Reserved semantics

Red, green, and amber mean labelled states. Ochre contour, lavender, sage, and red earth do not.

Stable while working

Loading must not reshuffle imagery or move the main action. The study does not animate.

Accessible by default

Keyboard, visible focus, labelled controls, sufficient contrast — requirements, not extras.

Few consistent decisions

Identity is the sage ground, the violet action, the ochre contour, and the serif nameplate — not brushwork on every control.

Core & state tokens

Decorative accents (no status meaning)

Assigned by position: ochre contour, lavender, sage, red earth. Editing these repaints the metric bands and the painted hero and footer. The wavy contour rules are fixed ochre, and the paintings stay the reference.

Chart colours

Charts take their own tokens, separate from the decorative accents above. The eight series hues come from the same three paintings, deepened or saturated until they work as data colours. They are assigned in this fixed order, slot 1 first, and never cycled. A ninth series folds into “Other”. The colour follows the entity: a filter that removes a series does not repaint the rest. Validated on the chalk chart surface: worst neighbouring pair ΔE 10.6 for colour-blind vision (target ≥ 8) and 19.8 for normal vision (floor 15). Scatter plots, maps and small multiples, where any two colours can touch, use only slots 1–3.

Ochre (2.5:1) and olive (2.9:1) fall below 3:1 on chalk, so any chart that uses slot 3 or 7 shows direct labels or a table view. Series text — values, labels, legends — stays in ink; the colour sits beside it as a mark.

Normal text needs 4.5:1; large text and meaningful UI components need 3:1. The input border is a sage-grey at about 3.8:1 on chalk, so inputs are never outlined in ochre. Recheck opacity and any text placed over a picture. These pairs do not certify the whole app.

Typography & icons

A display face for page identity, sans-serif for controls and data, tabular numerals for metrics. Essential supporting copy stays ≥12px; eyebrows only supplement a full title.

The serif is the nameplate on the wall; italic serif marks eyebrows, table heads and captions, like a label on a frame. Controls, tables, and explanations stay in the system sans. No remote fonts. Icons: one stroke family (Lucide-style below), nav ~18px, actions ~15–17px. An icon never replaces its text label except in compact navigation or a clearly named icon control.

§5

Layout & responsive behaviour

A persistent shell (navigation + topbar + main landmark), a shared page heading, and page content. Mount the shell once; provide a skip link to main. The ochre contour marks the bar, not the content.

Shell pattern (scaled)

≤650px: sidebar becomes an icon rail (with aria-labels) or a drawer — pick one. Wide tables scroll internally; never shrink type to fit a phone.

§6

Component patterns

Rendered with the set-study tokens. Adapt values; keep behaviour and accessibility guarantees. Controls stay sharp. Broken colour, from Clichy, is not a button style.

Buttons & controls

Link as button

Primary is garden violet with a thin ochre lip, not ochre itself. Focus is a violet outline with a soft ochre halo — the outline is the indicator, because ochre alone fails on chalk. Links navigate; buttons act. type="button" for non-submit buttons in forms. In-progress uses explicit text and blocks duplicate submits.

Status badges

Succeeded Active Pending Failed Queued Unknown → neutral

Colour always pairs with text. Success green is darker than the garden foliage and Brabant sage. Failure crimson is darker and cooler than the red earth, and it always says Failed. Unknown values get neutral styling; design new statuses explicitly.

Dialog

Native dialog + showModal(): labelled title, focus on close, Escape/backdrop close, focus restored, body scrolls. The dialog is another wall: opaque chalk under an ochre contour, not the painting.

§10

Accessibility baseline

This workbook itself demonstrates the baseline: skip link, visible focus, labelled controls, text+colour states, reduced-motion support, and a native dialog with focus management. The garden study adds no motion and puts no text on brushwork.

  • Keyboard reaches navigation, actions, filters, and table disclosures.
  • Skip link targets main; focus is a 3px violet outline, offset, with an ochre halo that is not the indicator.
  • Dialog title, focus placement, Escape, and focus restoration work.
  • Statuses and errors use text as well as colour; fields have programmatic labels.
  • Essential text meets contrast (see the live check in §3). Ochre is never used as text.
  • prefers-reduced-motion respected; the study does not animate in any case.
  • Hidden visible text (icon rail) keeps accessible names via aria-label.
